Capstone · Flagship project

Predicting hospital prices from 16 million NY SPARCS discharge records

For my Saint Louis University capstone, I worked with New York's SPARCS inpatient discharge data — roughly 16 million records — to model what drives hospital charges and costs. The work meant merging and cleaning multiple large files, engineering features from diagnosis, procedure, severity, and payer fields, and building a price-prediction model a hospital finance or planning team could actually reason about.

The dataNY SPARCS inpatient discharges — charges, costs, length of stay, diagnosis groups, and payment source at statewide scale.
The workData preparation and merging across files, cleaning and validation, exploratory analysis, and predictive modeling in Python.
The pointHospital prices look arbitrary from the outside. This project shows which clinical and payer factors actually move them.

Now building: the SPARCS Power BI dashboard

Notebooks prove analysis; dashboards prove communication. I'm rebuilding the capstone findings as an interactive Power BI dashboard so a hiring manager can see the story in thirty seconds — no code required.

Status: in progress. The data model is built from the cleaned capstone dataset. The published dashboard will be embedded on this page when it's live — the three views below are what it will contain.

VIEW 1

Charges vs. costs

How what hospitals charge compares with what care actually costs, broken down so the markup pattern is visible at a glance.

VIEW 2

Length of stay by diagnosis group

Which diagnosis groups keep patients longest — the utilization view an operations team uses to spot capacity pressure.

VIEW 3

Payment mix

The share of discharges and charges by payment category — who pays for inpatient care, and how the mix shifts by service line.

Slicers for year, region, and diagnosis group across every view.
